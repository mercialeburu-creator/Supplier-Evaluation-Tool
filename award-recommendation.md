Supplier Evaluation and Award Recommendation

1. Purpose

This document demonstrates how supplier evaluation results can be used to support a transparent, evidence-based procurement award recommendation.

Note: This is a hypothetical example for portfolio and demonstration purposes. The suppliers, quotations, and evaluation results are fictional.

| Supplier | Quoted Price (ZAR) |

| Metsi Engineering & Trading (Pty) Ltd | R126,000 |
| Kopano Industrial Solutions (Pty) Ltd | R115,000 |

3. Evaluation Methodology

The evaluation considers the following weighted criteria:

- Price: 30%
- Technical compliance: 25%
- Quality: 15%
- Delivery: 15%
- Regulatory and document compliance: 10%
- Customer service and support: 5%

The evaluation should be based on documented evidence, approved criteria, and consistent scoring rules. Mandatory requirements must be checked before a supplier is considered for award.

4. Preliminary Recommendation

Kopano Industrial Solutions (Pty) Ltd submitted the lowest quoted price of R115,000 in this illustrative example. However, the lowest price alone does not justify an award.

Before an award decision, the procurement team must verify the supplier's mandatory compliance, technical suitability, quotation validity, delivery commitments, commercial terms, and supporting evaluation evidence.

The weighted scores and supplier ranking must also be recalculated and independently checked against the evaluation matrix before any recommendation is approved.

5. Governance and Risk Controls

The following controls should be completed:

1. Confirm that all quotations are evaluated on a comparable basis.
2. Verify mandatory technical, legal, and regulatory requirements.
3. Validate all scores, weightings, calculations, and ranking.
4. Document material risks, clarifications, and deviations.
5. Declare and manage any conflicts of interest.
6. Retain the evaluation records and obtain the required approvals.

6. Conclusion

A defensible procurement award recommendation should demonstrate value for money, fairness, transparency, consistent application of the evaluation criteria, and an auditable decision trail.
